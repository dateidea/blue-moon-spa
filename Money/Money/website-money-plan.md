# The $6K in 16 Days Plan
*Qudsi, 92115 College Area, San Diego. 4-6 hrs/day. Goal: $6,000 by May 10, 2026.*

## The honest math first

Calendar: April 24 to May 10 is 16 days. At 4-6 hours/day you have roughly 80 working hours total.

$6,000 ÷ 80 hours = $75/hr blended. That is your minimum effective rate. If you take anything below that, you're losing money against the goal.

Possible paths to $6K:
- 3 clients × $2,000 (hard to close, slower pipeline)
- 4 clients × $1,500 (sweet spot, recommended)
- 6 clients × $1,000 (volume trap, eats time)

**Recommended target: 4 clients at $1,500 each.** Close 1 every 4 days. Build 1 every 3-4 days.

Reality check: first-time cold door-to-door conversion is typically 1-5%. To close 4 deals, plan on pitching 80-150 businesses in person. That is roughly 10 pitches per day. Doable in a 3-hour evening run plus weekend blocks.

## Why the "pay-per-call" model is NOT your 16-day play

The Ippei model you saw (rank a site, forward calls, charge per lead or commission) is real and lucrative. Single sites can earn $500-$3,000/month passively once ranked. BUT ranking a new site in competitive local SEO takes 60-120 days. You cannot deploy this model to hit May 10.

File it under "what you build starting Day 17." I'll give you the playbook at the end.

## Your 16-day play: done-for-you website sprints

### Tech stack decision

You told me the client needs to self-edit without code. That rules out pure HTML/React handoffs. Your options ranked:

1. **WordPress + Elementor (RECOMMENDED)**: Free platform, $5-15/mo hosting, dead simple drag-and-drop editor, gigantic plugin ecosystem. Clients have heard of it, which lowers sales friction. You keep control via admin access, they self-edit via Elementor.
2. **Webflow**: Prettier results, faster builds for you, but client pays $23/mo to Webflow on top of your fee. Better for design-forward brands.
3. **Wix / Squarespace**: Don't. Low perceived value. You will struggle to charge $1,500.

Go with WordPress + Elementor unless the client specifically wants Webflow design polish.

Sell three packages. Don't negotiate, just present and let them choose.

**STANDARD — $1,800** (push everyone here)

**PREMIUM — $2,800**

### On the maintenance question (2 months vs 1 month)

**Do 30 days.** Not 60. Here's why: every free-support day is a day you can't use to hit $6K. Industry norm is 14-30 days of post-launch bug fixes. Two months makes you look cheap and desperate, and it conditions the client to treat you as free labor.

Offer: "30 days of free fixes after launch. After that, you can hire me hourly at $100/hr or grab the $99/mo care plan and never think about it again." The care plan is where the real long-term money is. Three clients on a $99/mo plan = $297/mo recurring. A year in, that's $3,564 passive.

## The in-person pitch script

### Walking in
Don't ambush at 8 AM or noon rush. Go 10:30 AM, or 2-4 PM. Dress business casual. No tie. Bring your phone loaded with your 3 portfolio sites and a notepad.

### Opener (to front desk or whoever greets you)
"Hi, is the owner around? I had 3 specific ideas to help [business name] get more customers and wanted to share them with them directly. It'll take 3 minutes."

If owner not there: "When's a good time to catch them? I'll come back." Get their name and phone.

### Once in front of the owner
"Hey, I'm Qudsi. I build websites for local businesses here in San Diego. Before walking in, I pulled up your site/Google listing and noticed three things:

1. [One specific issue. Examples: 'Your site takes 6 seconds to load, Google penalizes that.' Or 'I searched plumber 92115 and you're on page 3 because your Google Business Profile is missing photos.' Or 'Your site doesn't have a click-to-call button on mobile, you're probably missing calls.']
2. [Second specific issue]
3. [Third specific issue]

I could fix all three and build you a site that actually brings calls in. My standard package is $1,800. I deliver in a week. I'd love to walk you through how it works. Can I pull up a site I built on my phone real quick?"

**Don't rush the close.** After showing portfolio, ask: "What questions do you have?" Then: "I can start Monday. Want to lock it in?"

If "let me think about it": "Totally fair. I'm booking 3 more clients this week. Can I text you tomorrow at 4 PM?" Get their number. Follow up.

### The critical pre-walk-in homework (do this the night before)

For every prospect, have these ready:
- Their current site URL (or note that they don't have one)
- Their Google Business Profile status (photos, review count, posts)
- One competitor in 92115 who is ranking above them
- Three specific fixes

This takes 5 minutes per prospect. It is the difference between "another spammer" and "this person did their homework." Nobody does this. It is your unfair advantage.

## Execution hacks

### Prospecting speed tricks
- Yelp: "plumber 92115", sort by review count descending, skip anything over 100 reviews (too big), skip anything 3.5 stars or below (angry customers, rough owner), target 15-60 reviews with 4+ stars. That is the sweet spot.
- Google Maps: search same terms, filter by "rated 4+ stars", zoom to College Area. Look for pins WITHOUT a linked website. Those are gold.
- Facebook Marketplace → Services: raw, unbranded operators live here
- Drive El Cajon Blvd between 54th and 73rd at 9 AM on a Saturday. Photograph work trucks with phone numbers. Call Monday.

### Instant credibility boosters
- Get 1 testimonial on video from your first close and load it on your phone. Show it in pitch 2 onward.
- Make a 60-second "before and after" reel on your phone. Show at pitches.
- Put your face on your service site. People buy from people.

### Close-rate boosters
- Offer "my first 5 clients in San Diego" framing with a 20% discount for testimonial rights. Gets stragglers off the fence.
- Free GBP audit as a leave-behind: print a 1-page sheet for each prospect showing their 3 specific issues. Costs you nothing, gets you the call back.
- Ask every close: "Who's another business owner in your circle who could use this?" Referrals close 3x faster than cold.

### The pay-per-call (lead gen) model, properly
Starting Day 17, pick ONE emergency-demand niche in San Diego. Emergency niches = high intent, high price per call.

Top niches (high revenue per call):
- Water damage / flood restoration: $200-$500 per qualified call
- Emergency plumbing: $50-$150 per call
- Tree removal (post-storm): $80-$200 per call
- Locksmith (lockout): $40-$100 per call
- Garage door repair: $30-$80 per call
- Roofing (storm damage): $100-$300 per call

Process:
1. Buy a domain like "sandiegoemergencyplumbers.com"
2. Build a niche site (you already know how)
3. Create GBP with a virtual office or partner's address (carefully, follow Google TOS or risk suspension)
4. Rank it via local SEO: citations, backlinks, GBP posts, content

6. Call a local plumber you built a site for: "I'm sending you real leads. Pay me $75 per booked job, or $800/month flat."

This is the asset model. One ranking site = $500-$3K/mo for years. Stack 5 of them = $2,500-$15,000/mo passive. This is the actual business you want to build.

## What could kill this plan

Being honest with you about the real risks:

1. **You don't pitch enough.** 4-6 hrs/day means ~3 hrs/day of actual selling after setup and builds. If you only pitch 5 businesses/day instead of 10, you'll miss the goal. This is the #1 failure mode. Pitching feels worse than building. Do it anyway.

2. **You over-deliver the first site.** Spending 30 hours on the first build instead of 10-15 will sink the timeline. Use templates. Use Elementor blocks. Don't design from scratch.

3. **You discount too fast.** If a prospect balks at $1,500, don't immediately drop to $800. Say: "$1,500 gets you the full 7-page build and first month of care. If you want to start smaller, I have a 3-page starter at $900 with no care plan." Anchor high, offer alternative, preserve margin.

4. **Scope creep.** Clients will ask for Instagram designs, logo redesigns, t-shirts. Say: "That's outside the website package. I can quote that separately after launch." No exceptions.

5. **Not collecting deposits.** 50% up front. No deposit, no work. If they won't put 50% down, they weren't going to pay the back end either.
