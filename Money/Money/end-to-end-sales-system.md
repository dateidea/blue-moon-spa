# The End-to-End Sales System
*From walk-in to launched site. No bluff. Every step scripted.*

---

## Part 1: What you're actually selling

Stop calling it a website. Call it a "customer acquisition system" or just "getting you more calls." The word "website" anchors them to Wix pricing. The word "calls" anchors them to the value of new customers.

Your actual deliverable at $1,500:

**Web property:** 7-page WordPress + Elementor site. Home, Services, Service Area, About, Contact, Reviews, 1 seed blog post. Mobile-first, under 3 second load on 4G. Click-to-call button fixed at bottom on mobile. Instant-delivery quote form.

**Google presence:** Google Business Profile claimed, verified, fully filled out (hours, service areas, 10 photos, 5+ FAQs, messaging enabled, services listed with prices). LocalBusiness schema markup on the site. Listed on Yelp, Bing Places, Apple Maps.

**Measurement:** Google Analytics + Search Console installed. Monthly email report for first 3 months showing: calls, form fills, which pages got traffic.

**Operations:** Post-job review request template (SMS and email). A 10-min video showing them how to edit pages. Written cheat sheet for common tasks.

**Guarantee:** 30 days of post-launch fixes at no cost. After that, hourly ($100/hr) or care plan ($99/mo).

This is 20-25 hours of actual work. At $1,500 you are working for $65/hr. That's below US median freelance rate but gets the deal closed.

## Part 2: The ROI pitch that justifies price

You never pitch the features. You pitch the math. Memorize this script and modify one variable per industry.

**For a plumber:** "A plumbing job in San Diego averages $400. If my site brings you ONE extra customer per month from Google, that's $4,800 in year one. The site cost $1,500. Your first-year ROI is 220%. And year two is free money, the site is already paid for. Is one extra customer a month realistic? Not really, most service businesses see 3-5 extra per month after getting found on Google."

**For HVAC:** Use $1,200 avg ticket. "One extra customer per month = $14,400 in year one. Site pays for itself on the first call."

**For a roofer:** $8,000 avg job. "Literally one extra customer in 18 months and you've 5x'd your money. This is not a decision you think about. This is a decision you make."

**For a tire shop:** $400 avg ticket. Same math as plumber.

**For a barber/nail/lash:** $40-$80 ticket, so harder. Don't sell on ROI, sell on competitive positioning: "Half the shops in College Area have no website. The owner's daughter on Instagram. You would immediately be the most professional-looking option in a 3-mile radius. That pulls new customers who haven't been to any shop yet, which is where growth comes from."

## Part 3: The full sales process, phase by phase

### PHASE 1: Homework (5-8 minutes per prospect, night before)

Non-negotiable. This is what separates you from every other sales pitch they get.

Open their Google Business Profile. Note:
- Photo count (under 10 is a weakness, over 25 is strong)
- Review count and average (under 20 reviews is huge opportunity)
- Last post date (no posts in 90 days is a weakness)
- Are they responding to reviews? Good or bad?
- Services listed? Hours complete? Service area set?

Search "[their service] 92115" on Google. Screenshot the top 3 results. These are their competitors.

Open their current website (if any) on your phone. Note:
- Load time (use web.dev/measure)
- Is there a click-to-call button visible without scrolling?
- Is the contact form easy to find?
- Any broken images or 404s?

Write 3 specific fixes on a sticky note. Examples:
- "Your site is 6 seconds to load on mobile. Google penalizes that."
- "Competitor X has 4x more photos on their GBP than you."
- "You are missing click-to-call on mobile. Every call from a phone requires the user to screenshot and manually dial."

This is your opening. Never walk in without it.

### PHASE 2: Walk-in (the first 90 seconds)

**Timing matters.** Avoid: opening hour (they are setting up), noon rush (they are slammed), closing hour (they are counting cash). Sweet spot: 10:30-11:30 AM and 2:00-4:00 PM.

**Dress:** Business casual. Clean collared shirt, clean shoes. No tie (too formal, makes you look like a salesperson). Polo + slacks is perfect. No backpack if avoidable, a slim portfolio or just your phone in hand.

**At the door:**

If there is a receptionist/employee:
> "Hi, is the owner around? I had 3 specific ideas to help [business name] get more customers online and wanted to share them with them directly. It will only take 3 minutes."

Key words: "specific ideas" and "3 minutes." You are not selling, you are offering. Most gatekeepers wave you through for 3 minutes.

If owner is not there:
> "When is a good time to catch them? I will come back."

Get their name if you can. "What is the owner's name so I can ask for them?" Write it down. Leave a card or sticky note that says "3 specific ideas to bring more customers. 3 minutes. Call/text me anytime." Your name and phone.

### PHASE 3: Discovery (10-15 minutes with owner)

**Do not pitch yet.** This is the mistake 90% of salespeople make. You walk in, you launch into the pitch, they shut down.

Instead, ask questions. Find the pain. The bigger the pain, the less the price matters.

Ask in this order:

1. "How do you get most of your customers right now?"
   - Listen for: referrals, walk-ins, Google, Yelp, ads.
   - If they say "mostly referrals," they are vulnerable. Referrals plateau. You can show them how to grow beyond their network.

2. "What is the biggest pain in getting more customers?"
   - Listen for: "competitors are bigger," "my website is ugly," "I do not have time," "I do not know marketing."
   - Take notes. This is your ammunition.

3. "Do you ever turn down work?"
   - If yes, they do not need more customers (now). Pivot to: "What if I could help you charge 20% more for the jobs you already take?" (This is a different conversation. More about branding/positioning.)
   - If no, you have a hungry buyer.

4. "When people call you, how many actually book?"
   - If they say "most," the website will have maximum ROI because you just need to increase call volume.
   - If they say "few," there is a conversion problem. Your site can improve this too.

5. "What happens if you get 2-3 extra customers per month?"
   - Get them to picture the money. "That would pay for my truck." "That would let me hire another guy." 
   - Whatever they say, you anchor the value at that dollar amount for them.

By the end of 15 minutes you know: their pain, their revenue per customer, their ambition, their current marketing spend. You have earned the right to pitch.

### PHASE 4: The pitch (5-10 minutes)

Now you pitch. Start with what you learned in discovery.

> "Based on what you just told me, here is what I would build. [Summarize their goal back to them.] The deliverable is three things:
>
> One: A website that loads in under 3 seconds on mobile with a click-to-call button your customers cannot miss. Most of your customers will find you on their phone at 10 PM. The site has to work for them.
>
> Two: Your Google Business Profile fully optimized. Right now [cite something specific from your homework: "you have 8 photos, your top competitor has 40"]. I fix that. Plus reviews, posts, service area, schema.
>
> Three: A system to collect reviews automatically. Every time you finish a job, an automated text goes out asking them to leave a review. Reviews are how Google decides who ranks."

Then the ROI math (from Part 2 above). Tailor to their industry.

Then show your portfolio. Open qudsisweets.com, qudsi.site, dateflo.com on your phone. Point out:
- Clean design
- Fast on mobile
- Clear call-to-action
- Easy to edit (if they ask)

Do not click through every page. 30 seconds per site, maximum. You are proving legitimacy, not showing off.

### PHASE 5: The close (3-5 minutes)

Transition words: "So here is how this works."

> "My standard package is $1,800. It includes everything I just described. I deliver in 7 to 10 days from when you send me content. Payment is 50% up front, 50% when I launch. I do not charge you anything for the first 30 days after launch if anything breaks.
>
> If you want to start smaller, I have a starter package at $900 that is a 3-page version. Same quality, just less real estate. You can upgrade later.
>
> Which one makes more sense for your business?"

Silence. Shut up. Let them answer.

**Possible responses:**

**"Let me think about it."**

> "Totally fair. Three things while you are thinking. First, my calendar is filling up, I am taking 3 more clients this week. Second, the sooner you start, the sooner the ROI starts. Third, I will text you tomorrow at 4 PM to answer any questions you think of tonight. Good?"

Get their number. Text them at 4 PM the next day. Include a screenshot of one "after" version of a site you built. Soft pressure.

**"That is too expensive."**

Never discount immediately. Say:

> "Compared to what? [Listen.] The $900 starter is 3 pages instead of 7. Or if budget is the issue, I also offer a monthly plan: $300 a month for 6 months, includes everything plus ongoing maintenance. No upfront cost. You are paying from the revenue it brings you."

(Monthly plan math: $300 × 6 = $1,800. You get $300 now, then $300/month. Easier to say yes to. Works great for prospects who "have the revenue but not the cash today.")

**"I have a website already."**

> "Who built it? [Listen.] I took a look at it. Here are 3 things I think could be costing you calls right now. [Say the 3 fixes from your homework.] A redesign with me is $1,500 and I carry over any good content from your current site."

**"I need to talk to my partner/wife/husband/accountant."**

> "Smart. Here is what would help them decide: I will send you a one-page proposal tonight with scope, price, timeline, and ROI math. You show it to them. I will text you tomorrow at noon to answer questions. What is your email?"

Send a proposal that night. Plain PDF. Scope, price, timeline, 3 case studies or your portfolio. Template below.

**"Send me info."**

Do not fall for this. They are saying no. Instead:

> "I can send info but honestly, sites like mine are customized to your business. A generic info packet will not tell you much. What would be more useful: if I text you a 2-minute video in the next hour walking through exactly what I would build for you specifically. Is that okay?"

Record a 2-min loom or just a phone video. Show their site, say what you would change, show the ROI math. If they watch it, they are still in.

**"YES, let's do it."**

> "Great. I will email you a simple contract tonight. Sign it and send $900 deposit via Zelle [or Venmo or Stripe link]. Once that's in, I will send you a questionnaire and we kick off Monday. My email is [yours]. What's yours?"

Exchange contact info. Write the deposit amount and date on a business card, hand it to them. Physical artifact = they feel committed.

### PHASE 6: Same-day follow-up (regardless of outcome)

Within 2 hours of the pitch, text them:

> "Hey [name], Qudsi here. Really appreciated the conversation today. Sending over [proposal / portfolio link / the loom video we discussed]. Text me anytime with questions. Looking forward to it."

Tomorrow at 10 AM:

> "Morning [name]. Any questions come up after yesterday?"

Day 3:

> "Hey [name], checking in. I have two other potential clients closing this week. Wanted to reserve your project slot for next week. Ready to move forward?"

Day 5 (final push):

> "[Name], last check in before I move on. Still interested? Totally fine either way, just want to respect your time."

After Day 5, let them go. Circle back in 60 days if you want.

### PHASE 7: Onboarding (first 48 hours after deposit)

The moment the deposit hits, send this email:

**Subject:** Welcome aboard — your site kicks off Monday

**Body:**
> Hey [name],
>
> Excited to build your site. Deposit received, confirmed $900.
>
> Three things I need from you by Wednesday 6 PM to stay on timeline:
>
> 1. Fill out this 10-minute questionnaire: [link to Google Form or Typeform]
> 2. Send me: your logo (if you have one), 10-15 photos of your work, a list of your services with brief descriptions
> 3. Confirm the domain you want: do you already own one? Or do you want me to register [suggested].com for you?
>
> Timeline:
> - Monday: I send you a sitemap and wireframe to approve
> - Tuesday-Friday: I build
> - Saturday: You review the draft, send one round of changes
> - Following Monday: Launch
>
> Questions? Text 619-XXX-XXXX anytime.
>
> Qudsi

The questionnaire asks:
- Business description in 2 sentences
- Top 3 services
- Price range per service (optional)
- Service area (zip codes)
- Hours
- 5 things that make you different from competitors
- 2-3 competitors whose sites you like (for design reference)
- 3-5 happy customers (name, one-line testimonial, permission to use)
- Preferred colors
- Phone number and email for contact form

This is your entire content brief. Without it, you cannot start.

### PHASE 8: Build (7-10 days)

**Day 1 (Monday):** Sitemap + wireframe via Figma link. Send them. They approve or ask for changes. Lock within 24 hours.

**Day 2-4 (Tue-Thu):** Build in Elementor. Use pre-made kits to move fast. Do not design from scratch. Industries that use Elementor Pro have tons of ready-to-customize templates.

**Day 5 (Fri):** First draft review. Send them a staging URL. Tell them:
> "One round of changes is included. Please gather all feedback in one reply by Monday noon. After that round, additional changes are $100/hr."

This prevents endless tweaks.

**Day 6 (Sat):** GBP work. Claim, verify, fill out everything, upload photos, set services with prices.

**Day 7 (Sun):** Schema markup, analytics, search console, final SEO pass.

**Day 8 (Mon):** Apply their feedback. Final QA on mobile, desktop, 3 browsers.

**Day 9 (Tue):** Launch. Point domain. Send invoice for remaining $900.

**Day 10 (Wed):** Walkthrough call. Hand them the cheat sheet. Get them into the admin panel. Record a Loom of the 5 most common edits they might want to make.

### PHASE 9: The 30-day post-launch (where the REAL revenue starts)

**Week 1:** Monitor. Fix anything they report within 24 hours. These are reputational, not billable.

**Week 2:** Send a report.

> "Hey [name], first week update. You got [X] unique visitors, [Y] phone calls, [Z] form submissions. Here are the top 3 pages people viewed: [list]. Want to jump on a 15-min call to go over it?"

**Week 3:** Ask for referral and review.

> "Hey [name], two quick asks. One: could you leave me a 1-minute Google review at [link]? It would mean a lot. Two: do you know another business owner who could use what I built for you? I pay $100 referral bonus on any closed deal."

**Week 4:** Pitch the care plan.

> "Hey [name], your 30 days of free support is wrapping up this Friday. Two options going forward: $99/month care plan (covers hosting, backups, security updates, 1 hour of content changes per month), or hourly at $100/hr if something comes up. Most clients do the monthly, it's cheaper than calling me once a quarter. Want to lock in the care plan?"

60% of clients will say yes to the care plan. That's your real business: $99 × N clients/month forever.

### PHASE 10: Referral loop (the $0-cost growth machine)

Every care-plan client gets this every 90 days:

> "Quick check-in. Site running well? Anything to change? Also, do you know other business owners who need what I built for you? $100 referral on any closed deal. Text me names and I will handle the outreach."

Referrals close 3x faster and at 2x the price of cold leads.

## Part 5: The uncomfortable truths

**You will get rejected 90-97% of the time.** That is the actual math of cold in-person sales. Do not take it personally. A 95% rejection rate means you close 5 out of every 100 pitches. At 10 pitches per day you close 1 every other day. At that rate you hit 4 closes in 8 days.

**Most owners will stall, not reject.** "Let me think," "I need to talk to my wife," "email me." These are not nos. These are soft nos. Your follow-up discipline is what converts them. People who follow up for 5 days close 60% more than people who follow up once.

**The first pitch is always the worst pitch.** You will sound nervous. You will forget lines. You will botch the ROI math. That is fine. Pitch 5 before you pitch 10. You will be noticeably better by pitch 15. Do not judge your results until you have done 50 pitches.

**The owner's bank account is not your problem.** If they cannot afford $1,500, they are not your client. Do not convince someone to buy something they cannot pay for. That ends in refund requests and 1-star reviews.

**Price resistance is a pain signal, not a truth signal.** When they say "too expensive," what they usually mean is "I don't yet see the value." If your ROI math is solid and specific, price resistance disappears. If they still push back after honest math, they are not serious buyers and you move on.
